* What is true

- The landing (=_pages/helios.md=) is unchanged. Its interval is three films —
  helix (ribbon), spiral (mass), tree (echo) — screen-blended under one sky.
- =/helios-interval= is the probe, and it no longer rehearses the helix. It walks
  *the morphology* (below) on the dojo's own runtime — the real language, ambients
  and all — from a spike pin (=_data/hatch.yml=, =release: false=; see
  =design/hatch.org=).
- The site turtle, =assets/js/turtling/=, is a different and older 2D runtime: what
  =/turtle= runs. It has no ambients, so a rehearsal on it could only approximate
  movement three. That page is no longer the shape of this one.
- The shell (=localhost:4001/shell=) is the true drawing: a WebGL ribbon,
  =WebGLRenderer({ alpha: true })=. The black in that picture is the page behind
  the canvas; the program never paints a ground.
- The dojo runtime is not a library. =turtle.js= imports the weave, the kernel,
  the scheduler, the compositor, and three.js. There is no exported hatch yet;
  that path is =design/hatch.org= and it is entirely unbuilt.
- The people in /In who we become/ are films. They stay films.

* The world

A world is one figure, named by a program.

|       | film              | world                          |
|-------+-------------------+--------------------------------|
| ground| encoded black     | never painted                  |
| clock | the decoder       | the program's own =wait=       |
| bytes | the pictures      | the text, plus one runtime paid once |
| leaves| the player unbinds| the loop pauses                |
| failure| the poster       | the film, behind               |

One canvas, one loop, for every program in the interval. Not a canvas per figure,
and not a copy of the shell per figure. Browsers cap WebGL contexts; the win is
not bandwidth, it is that the stroke is the only pixel and the figures share a
clock.

Reduced motion draws the finished figure once and stops.

* The morphology

There is *one source program, not three*, whose structure stays recognisable
while its body accumulates three finite movements.

| movement | act        | content                                                        |
|----------+------------+----------------------------------------------------------------|
| 1        | see        | draw =polygon 4= on radius =r=                                 |
| 2        | play with  | a finite exhaustion sequence on the same circumcircle, =side = 2rsin[pi/sides]= |
| 3        | breathe    | spawn one ambient from each final polygon vertex; each pursues the next |

Conditions, part of the content and not decoration:

- The exhaustion polygons share one centre and one radius. Increasing =sides=
  changes the polygon, not its circumcircle.
- The sequence is explicitly finite. No recursion is allowed to terminate only
  because a runtime cap was reached.
- =wait= supplies the temporal joints. Page timers do not advance the program.
- Movement 3 begins from the actual final vertices produced by movement 2. The
  page does not reconstruct or inject those coordinates.
- Agent identity and successor order are deterministic.
- Cross-ambient observation uses the language's address/weave semantics.
- The canvas is not cleared between movements: the earlier constructions remain
  as sediment beneath the later one.
- The whole performance is bounded. Caps are a fault boundary, not the intended
  stopping condition.

Do not specify "byte-identical skeleton across three programs": there are no
three programs to compare. The invariant is one source artifact whose execution
passes through three movement boundaries.

Movement 3 ends; it does not seed another generation. For a one-viewport landing
act a finite becoming is stronger than permanent background motion: it arrives,
transforms, and leaves a still figure. The runtime can stop its render loop,
=finished= can resolve, the code column can finish, and a reader who stays is not
made to watch the same claim indefinitely. Use a bounded pursuit duration or step
count chosen so the agents are visually converged; if an epsilon test is also
used, keep a hard maximum step count.

Cycling costs more than another =loop=: generation state, ambient teardown and
reseeding, a rule for whether old trails accumulate or clear, a permanent
scheduler, repeated code-column beats, another source of determinism bugs — and
if converged positions become the next polygon, that inheritance must be in the
language program, not in page JavaScript. If repetition is later wanted,
re-entering the act may create a fresh hatch and perform the same finite program
again. That is replay, not a second law inside the mathematics.

The shell's morphology is *not written*: no repository holds these three
movements as one PaperLang program. =codex/fragments/the-whirlpool.org= has the
four mice on a square; =the-chase.org= has the fox and the hare. Neither is the
morphology.

* What the score is, and where the next attempt attaches

The score kindles the program's own lines from the runtime's beat stream: unread,
warm once walked, lit while the walk is on it — exactly one line lit at a time —
and, on the beat, the rest that joint closed is struck; a body's far =end= is
ticked as its opener's pair, and the program's last =end= ends in an afterglow.
Every one of those is LIGHT: no token's ink moves with the walk.

Two things were built here and then reconsidered for the same reason — both are
page-side answers to a question the language owns.

- *Syntax highlighting.* Kept, in one file with no dependencies:
  =assets/js/home/helios-plang-tokens.js=. It is a tokenizer, not a parser and not
  a highlighter: given a line it returns tokens and a kind, and knows no DOM. The
  page wraps those kinds in its own classes and =components/helios.css= gives each
  table and the parser's block words — so it will drift when the language grows;
  it HAD drifted, missing =for=, =draw= and =do= and misplacing =fn=, which left
  =do= and =end= inked as names and the language's own joints unsayable. Corrected
  word for word against the pinned bytes. What keeps it honest is that it is one
  replaceable file (the shape a dojo-published tokenizer would have), and that the
  page asserts two things about what it produces: gestures are warm, names are
  cool.

  *The rule that makes it legible*: a token's KIND gives its hue and never
  changes; the walk gives LIGHT and never hue. So the ignite does not repaint the
  line — it lights it, and a gesture stays a gesture whether the walk is on it or
  a hundred lines past it. The current line is marked by position as well (a caret
  in the column's margin), because with hue spoken for the "here" had to move to a
  channel of its own. Both are asserted in =design/probe/interval.mjs=.

- *Joint recovery, taken as far as the page may go.* A beat never reports the
  =wait= that closes it, so the page reads the rest from the verse it renders: a
  depth scan pairs every =do= with its =end=, and a beat strikes the first =wait=
  line after the last statement the beat walked, inside the body that contains
  it. This is NOT the removed arithmetic — no clock is consulted, and the verse's
  own text names the rest, where the arithmetic matched a =time= gap against a
  duration literal and went silent wherever two waits shared one. It is still the
  page standing in for a fact the executor holds, and still limited: it is exact
  for a body whose rests follow its statements, and it misnames a branch that
  skips its rest. The fix remains one span on the wait effect, added to the beat
  it closes; =restOf= and =restLength= in =assets/js/home/helios-interval.js= are
  the two functions it deletes.

*The seam that stays.* Each verse line is a =<span>= the page renders once and
never rewrites, and the classes it asserts on them are =--done=, =--lit=, =--rest=
and =--pair= (plus =--afterglow= at the end). So a highlighter attaches by adding
children inside those spans, and nothing in the driver has to change. The
scaffolding that stays is the rest:
=design/vendor.mjs= and =design/probe/hatch.py= for the pin, =design/probe/interval.mjs=
for this page's contract, and the checks in =design/probe/= for the site.

* The section

The interval is the room between the ticker and who we become. It is not a second
cinema and not an editor.

Composition, already tried and kept:

- Three objects, not a field of plates. The helix is the ribbon, entering from the
  corner and leaving the frame. The spiral and the tree overlap it, smaller. The
  spring, the white waves, the wheel, and the orbit ring collided or added a
  second law. They are out until a program earns a place.
- One sky across the interval and who we become. Violet and ember cross the
  boundary, as in =design/prior/helios.jpg= and =design/prior/tiger.jpg=. No
  ridge. Each act must not paint its own climate.
- The code reads as a column of verse in that night, not as a terminal. No
  command deck, no disciples, no CodeMirror.
- The score is the program, not a transcript of it: the line being walked is
  kindled, in execution order, from the program's own clock. Reading order and
  walking order are two different orders, and the picture must not lie about it.

* What is not the path

- Keying the mp4, or converting it to a gif. H.264 has no alpha; a gif cannot be
  sought and cannot be released.
- A canvas that plays the film and erases black each frame. Same picture, second
  machine.
- Copying =dojo/assets/js/turtling/= onto =/turtle= or into this site. The
  terminal arrives through the imports.
- Replacing =/turtle=. It is a standing IDE on the old runtime.
- Putting the performance on the landing before the probe matches the shell.
- Vendoring by hand. =design/vendor.mjs= is the only writer of the pin, and it
  refuses a release the seven checks do not pass; a hand-copied bundle is what
  =release: false= exists to mark, not a way to ship. See =design/hatch.org=.

* Order

1. *Judge the content on the probe.* — done, in the rehearsal's dialect:
   =/helios-interval= walks the morphology, one source, three movements, the
   verse kindled from the same clock. Measured: 52 lines, a logical score of
   11.8 s against the film's 11.625 s, no fallback byte requested on success, and
   one finished figure under reduced motion. It proves the content and the clock.
   It cannot prove the chase.
2. *Publish, pin, vendor the host.* — half-built. The site side exists and is
   exercised against a fixture: =design/vendor.mjs= writes =_data/hatch.yml= and
   =design/probe/hatch.py= checks it. The dojo side does not: no =host-v1= entry,
   no manifest, no conformance fixture, no =v0.5= tag. Until it exists the page
   runs a spike pin (=release: false=) and the path is =design/hatch.org=.
3. *The other programs, then the landing.* — blocked on 2. A figure without a
   program stays a film. Spiral, tree, and wheel are candidates only once each
   exists as commands the hatch can run.

Each step is judged on the probe before the next begins. The landing moves last.
