{%- comment -%} The navigation. One component, every page. THREE THINGS IN TWO FIXED LAYERS ON DARK SURFACES: __chrome the brand, burger, and mobile menu. __links the desktop plates, kept at the fixed --nav-ink colour. The links and chrome keep separate stacking positions. On a dark surface the bar overlays content, but its text colour does not respond to video frames or photographs. On a light surface the bar remains in flow. ORDER IS A CONTRACT. `[data-nav-mark]` must be the first focusable element in the bar — design/probe/helios.mjs asserts it is the first keyboard stop, and design/probe/identity.mjs asserts that every point over the drawn wordmark's box belongs to that link. So the chrome comes first, the plates keep the `.folio-nav__plates` class the probes query, and the burger sits with the brand. The mark is the banner from `_data/nav.yml`, not a text wordmark, because the same element has to be the logo on the pages that show a logo AND the invisible hit area under the drawn identity on the ones that draw one. Those hide it with `[data-nav-mark] { opacity: 0 }` — see components/helios.css and components/evening.css — and the link's own box is then extended by a pseudo-element to cover the art. Nothing here knows which page it is on. The panel is the same four marks nav.js has always bound. It is `display: none` above the breakpoint in components/nav.css, and nav.js closes it at the SAME breakpoint; those two numbers must stay equal or a resize can leave the body scroll-locked with no visible way out. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign nav = site.data.nav -%} {%- assign tone = page.nav_tone | default: nav.default_tone | default: 'light' -%}

The image inside keeps its full width, so the emblem does not move as the box narrows; only the word is cut away. {%- endcomment -%}
