{%- comment -%} The navigation. One component, every page. THREE THINGS IN THREE LAYERS, and the split is not decoration: __chrome the brand and the burger. Never blended — the banner is artwork, and a coloured raster under `difference` turns cyan wherever the backdrop is light. __links the plates. Blended on the overlaying surfaces, so the ink is derived from whatever is actually behind it. panel the mobile menu. Never blended, because an opaque panel under `difference` stops being a panel at all — it becomes a window onto the page and the links on it are unreadable. WHY THE LAYERS EXIST. `mix-blend-mode` blends an element's whole subtree into the backdrop, and a fixed ancestor confines that backdrop to the ancestor's own group. Measured in Chromium on /beginnings, four ways: baseline faint; blend on `.folio-nav__plates` alone, nothing; the same with the nav's `z-index: auto`, the plates vanished behind the content; blend on the whole nav, legible but the mobile panel became a window. So the links must BE the fixed layer, and everything that must not invert has to live outside it. One element cannot do both jobs, which is why there are two. The blend is applied only on `data-tone="dark"` (see components/nav.css): those are the surfaces where the bar overlays content. On a light surface the bar is in flow over the page's own ground, where a blend would invert against a background that is already known and fixed, and tint the banner for nothing. ORDER IS A CONTRACT. `[data-nav-mark]` must be the first focusable element in the bar — design/probe/helios.mjs asserts it is the first keyboard stop, and design/probe/identity.mjs asserts that every point over the drawn wordmark's box belongs to that link. So the chrome comes first, the plates keep the `.folio-nav__plates` class the probes query, and the burger sits with the brand. The mark is the banner from `_data/nav.yml`, not a text wordmark, because the same element has to be the logo on the pages that show a logo AND the invisible hit area under the drawn identity on the ones that draw one. Those hide it with `[data-nav-mark] { opacity: 0 }` — see components/helios.css and components/evening.css — and the link's own box is then extended by a pseudo-element to cover the art. Nothing here knows which page it is on. The panel is the same four marks nav.js has always bound. It is `display: none` above the breakpoint in components/nav.css, and nav.js closes it at the SAME breakpoint; those two numbers must stay equal or a resize can leave the body scroll-locked with no visible way out. {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign nav = site.data.nav -%} {%- assign tone = page.nav_tone | default: nav.default_tone | default: 'light' -%}
