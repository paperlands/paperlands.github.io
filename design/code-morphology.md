# A small instruction, a world inside it

## Status and scope

**Design proposal, not an implemented or ratified contract.** This proposes the triangle → recursive triangle → malleable figure as an alternative to the square/exhaustion/pursuit content in `design/interval.org`. It does not silently supersede that document, `design/hatch.org`, or the homepage.

The operator reports that current Paperlang supports playable and malleable figures. Treat that as the creative premise. The website's installed spike and documented host boundary are a separate compatibility question: `_data/hatch.yml` says `release: false`; the currently used host door is `createHatch`, `play`, `onLine`, `dispose`. A figure-input protocol is not established by that website code. Do not mistake an older site pin for the capabilities of current Paperlang, or current language capabilities for an already-integrated site API.

No production code is changed by this proposal. Existing uncommitted work is left alone.

### Playable evidence now available

Three design-only treatments of A are implemented at `design/iterations/13-morphology.html`: the score, the afterimage, and the inward garden. See `design/iterations/morphology-README.md` for preview commands, measured behaviours, and remaining questions.

Browser checks confirm that the installed spike accepts the guarded recursive definition and native reactive `let X` figure construction; actual mouse and touch drags change the geometry. The long verbs remain unsupported by this pin, so that beat is an explicit notation comparison. The probe uses a same-origin isolated viewport for the window-sized canvas/overlay; this does not ratify a production host boundary or change the homepage. The latest probe passes 54 browser assertions.


## The proposition

Not a tutorial with animated transitions. **A little film of an idea becoming more powerful, which eventually passes into the visitor's hands.**

A movement becomes a shape. Repeated movements become a rule. The rule acquires a name, a variable, and the ability to invoke itself. A limit allows it to return. A point on the page becomes its living input.

The emotional sequence:

> I recognise that. → I can follow that. → That little thing did all this? → I can change it.

The important surprise is not merely that the drawing grows complicated. It is that its description first gets simpler, and then a tiny addition generates a world of detail.

Working public title: **A world inside a triangle.**
Working production name: **The living stroke.**

### The Goethean constraint

Here, morphology means recognising a common organisation through transformations. It does not mean applying a liquid-morph filter, illustrating biological evolution, or decorating the piece with leaves.

The visitor should be able to point to something in the later state and recognise what it was in the earlier state. Preserve the gesture, the token, the corner, the heading, the scale relation, or the controlling point. When an identity changes, make that change explicit.

The website is not making a scientific argument about Goethe's historical botany. His observational approach and expansion/contraction are artistic and structural priors.

## 1. The visual ancestry

These are mechanism references, not a board of attractive screenshots. Research below is based on retrieved artist, museum, archive, and explanatory texts; it is not a frame-by-frame analysis of all the films. Shot-level timing remains a prototype task. Reference links are not permissions to reproduce footage, artwork, or music.

### Goethe: metamorphosis, not a slideshow of specimens

Zemplén Gábor's account distinguishes comparing many plants from following the transformations of one plant. It discusses expansion/contraction, common organisation, and the significance of reversibility.

**Borrow:** one object under successive transformations; expansion followed by contraction; returning to an earlier form as a way of seeing the relation.
**Resist:** a compulsory march toward a supposedly superior final form. The original triangle should remain beautiful and worth returning to.
**Application:** let the repeated commands unfold, then contract into a loop; let that loop unfold again on request. Moving backwards is interpretation, not merely navigation.

Sources: [Form as Movement in Goethe's The Metamorphosis of Plants](https://hps.elte.hu/~zemplen/goethemorph.html); [the poem, in an English translation](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Works_of_J._W._von_Goethe/Volume_9/The_Metamorphosis_of_Plants). The latter is a poem, not the 1790 botanical treatise.

### M. C. Escher: Metamorphosis II, 1939–40

The museum describes a continuous series beginning with the word metamorphosis, passing through tessellations and recognisable forms, and returning to the original lettering. It is a cycle, rather than a gallery of unrelated endpoints.

**Borrow:** local correspondence across a surprising transformation; continuity between language and image; a form that can be read in both directions.
**Resist:** relentless visual cleverness. Every transition here must explain something specific about this program.
**Application:** the repeated instruction pairs retain their identities while becoming the body of a loop. Words do not crossfade wholesale into unrelated words.

Source: [Museum Escher in The Palace](https://escherinhetpaleis.nl/en/about-escher/masterpieces/metamorphose-ii).

### Osvaldo Cavandoli: La Linea

The animation is a useful minimalist ancestor: a world organised around a drawn line, rather than a collection of panels and objects. The retrieved Expanded Animation entry presents it as a less-is-more example and links a contemporary successor.

**Borrow:** radical economy; continuity of the drawing surface; the sense that a small graphic element can carry a whole world.
**Resist:** adding a mascot and comic narrative that compete with the mathematics.
**Application:** the moving tip of the stroke is enough of a protagonist. Its heading must be visible when turning matters.

Source: [Expanded Animation: La Linea](https://expandedanimation.net/2011/10/25/la-linea/).

### Norman McLaren: Pas de deux, 1968

TIFF documents backlit dancers against black, with individual frames exposed up to eleven times through an optical printer. A gesture becomes a visible family of temporal echoes.

**Borrow:** a faint previous state as memory, making change readable; multiplicity growing from one recognisable gesture.
**Resist:** leaving every echo permanently visible. That would obscure the actual state and turn recursion into luminous mush.
**Application:** a previous length may remain briefly as a labelled outline; a recursive child may be compared with its parent. Such overlays are explanatory comparisons, not claims that those ghosts belong to the executed drawing.

Sources: [TIFF Canadian Film Encyclopedia](https://cfe.tiff.net/content/films/pas-de-deux); [NFB film page](https://www.nfb.ca/film/pas_de_deux/).

### Oskar Fischinger: Studies and Motion Painting No. 1

The Fischinger Archive describes the Studies as distinct visual problems involving simple shapes and rhythmic relationships. It describes Motion Painting No. 1 as documenting successive brush strokes, with a relationship to musical structure rather than tight note-by-note synchronisation.

**Borrow:** a visual phrase returning in another register; accumulation with discernible organisation; the drawing process as content.
**Resist:** equating every event with a beat, or turning the website into a music visualiser.
**Application:** forward, turn, forward, turn becomes a recognisable phrase. The loop reuses that phrase; recursion contains phrases inside phrases.

Sources: [Fischinger Archive biography](https://www.oskarfischinger.org/OFBioLong.htm); [Centre Pompidou: Studie nr.5/R5](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/ressources/oeuvre/cLrj5qE).

### Len Lye: Free Radicals, 1958, revised 1979

The Len Lye Foundation describes scratched black film, reducing the medium to light in darkness. Its material character comes from making the marks, not from applying a vintage treatment afterwards.

**Borrow:** a living, materially specific stroke against a quiet dark field.
**Resist:** constant scratch jitter, film damage, strobing, or decorative lightning.
**Application:** exact geometric centreline; a restrained, stable optical edge. Irregularity can live in stroke coverage and the atmosphere, never in the mathematical coordinates.

Source: [The Len Lye Foundation](https://www.lenlyefoundation.com/films/free-radicals/33/).

### Paul Klee: Pedagogical Sketchbook, 1925

The Bauhaus Archive describes a treatment of line, shape, surface, and colour that joins diagrammatic reasoning with craft and intuition.

**Borrow:** geometry as an activity and a formation, not just a completed diagram; small annotations that reveal a movement.
**Resist:** turning the site into a facsimile Bauhaus workbook.
**Application:** show the turn as an exterior-angle arc at the moving tip, before naming the whole triangle.

Source: [Bauhaus Archive](https://www.bauhaus.de/en/research/publications/bauhausbuecher-2-paul-kleepedagogical-sketchbook/).

### Charles and Ray Eames: Powers of Ten, 1977

The Eames Foundation frames the film as continuity and change through scale, anchored in a familiar scene.

**Borrow:** an intelligible scale relationship and a stable reference.
**Resist:** an obligatory infinite zoom, which hides the relation to the original triangle.
**Application:** briefly show 500 → 250 → 125 alongside one parent-child chain. Keep the outer triangle fixed during depth play.

Source: [Eames Foundation](https://eamesfoundation.org/work/powers-of-ten-and-the-relative-size-of-things-in-the-universe/).

### Johnny Kelly: The Seed, 2008

Motionographer documents a combination of 2D animation and papercut stop motion following an apple seed's journey.

**Borrow:** a graphic idea can change its material treatment without losing its narrative identity; handcraftedness can emerge from a precise process.
**Resist:** making a literal plant, adding an entire papercraft environment, or simulating stop motion while the visitor is dragging.
**Application:** authored transitions can have a slightly stepped, deliberate rhythm; direct manipulation stays fluid and immediate.

Source: [Motionographer](https://motionographer.com/2008/11/27/johnny-kellys-seed/).

### D'Arcy Thompson: a supporting graphic prior

MacTutor shows a fish over a coordinate grid, transformed by systematically deforming that grid into another recognisable fish.

**Borrow:** show the transformation law, not just the endpoints.
**Resist:** implying that our recursion is a deformation grid or that visual resemblance proves biological causation.
**Application:** a short visible chain from X's position to depth to the generated detail. The relation is the graphic, not an ornamental connector.

Source: [Mathematics of Transformations](https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Darcy/transformation/).

### The shortest useful reference set

For actual art direction, start with **Escher + McLaren + Len Lye + Klee**. Goethe supplies the compositional principle. The others solve particular questions; they need not all become visible stylistic influences.

## 2. Five rounds of design dialectic

These are deliberately opposed readings of the proposal, not claims of independent external reviews or completed user tests.

### Round 1 — The director versus the visitor

**Director:** stage a continuous transformation with exquisite timing. Do not interrupt its spell with controls.
**Visitor:** I missed the transition. If it cannot wait for me, I am watching someone else understand.
**Resolution:** retain cinematic continuity, but give the visitor the right to pause, reverse, and revisit. Motion is a demonstration, not the authority over progression. Never advance because a reading timer expired.
**Design change:** stable source states, explicit Previous/Next controls, and a reversible morphology. Native page scroll remains free.

### Round 2 — The morphologist versus the mathematician

**Morphologist:** let everything stretch, bend, and grow into everything else.
**Mathematician:** several of these edits do not change the picture at all. Inventing a geometric morph would explain the wrong thing.
**Resolution:** distinguish different kinds of becoming. Syntax contraction is not geometric deformation. Naming is not drawing. A continuous visual bridge need not pretend to be a valid intermediate program.
**Design change:** the triangle stays perfectly still during repeat → loop, long verbs → aliases, and encapsulation in a definition. The stillness becomes the evidence.

### Round 3 — The spectacle-maker versus the curious child

**Spectacle-maker:** open with the most dazzling fractal, then explain how it works.
**Curious child:** then the little line is just a tedious way back to something you already showed me.
**Resolution:** do not spend the climax before the visitor has a relation to it. Give them an incomplete gesture first and let its completion become satisfying.
**Design change:** one side and an inviting heading appear first. The recursive world is earned by a tiny, visible addition, not used as background decoration.

### Round 4 — The tactile designer versus the engineer

**Tactile designer:** make detail increase smoothly as the hand moves. The form should feel elastic.
**Engineer:** recursive depth is an integer; arbitrary interpolation can look like a different mathematical rule. Recomputing everything on every pointer event can break the feeling anyway.
**Resolution:** smooth hand, discrete mathematical consequences. Make the snapping relation visible. Do not counterfeit fractional recursion.
**Design change:** X moves continuously along a bounded rail; a small snapped witness marks the depth; only a change in depth requests new geometry. When a rapid drag crosses several thresholds, the witness still reports the selected integer clearly; it does not force the visitor through an obsolete animation queue.

### Round 5 — The romantic versus the editor

**Romantic:** add a plant, a paper cabinet, atmospheric sound, a zoom into infinity, and a gallery of generative worlds.
**Editor:** the tiny program is already the magic object. Every extra world competes with its causality.
**Resolution:** make one form unusually well. Distinctiveness should come from its temporal behaviour and the final handoff, not a surplus of motifs.
**Design change:** one persistent stage, one evolving source, one controlling point. No new asset-heavy environment. Prototype the difficult loop contraction and the first touch before building the entire journey.

## 3. The morphological grammar

| Kind of transformation | What changes | What must survive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Extension | A partial walk becomes a closed walk | Origin, first edge, heading convention |
| Repetition | One instruction pair acquires siblings | The pair's visual identity and order |
| Contraction | Explicit copies become a loop | Meaning, output, the body of one pair |
| Lexical shortening | forward/left become fw/lt | Meaning and the entire drawing |
| Encapsulation | A body acquires a name and a call | Body identity; definition versus execution |
| Parameterisation | A literal becomes an argument | The relation between call-site value and length |
| Self-reference | A rule invokes a smaller instance | Local heading, halving relation, call/return |
| Delimitation | A stopping condition enables return | The recursive rule; visible decreasing steps |
| Coupling | Position becomes a live input | An inspectable path from X.x to depth to detail |

There are nine canonical source states, not nine unrelated demo programs. Persist semantic token identities across their presentation. The current interval contract describes one source executed through three movements; this proposal instead describes a family of source versions. That is an explicit content/host-policy decision to ratify, not a word change in the existing contract.

### Motion rules

1. Keep what is unchanged anchored. An unchanged token does not fade out and reappear.
2. Draw translational motion with approximately constant speed; show rotation as rotation, not a teleporting cursor.
3. Contraction may ease gently. Pointer tracking must not lag behind an easing function.
4. Introduce only one conceptual change per rewrite.
5. Explain with a brief comparison, then remove the scaffolding.
6. Separate rewrite choreography from execution. Only runtime evidence may claim which instruction is executing.
7. No full-screen flashes, strobing verbs, or infinite idle animation. Use a brief emphasis or change in light.
8. A camera move is not a program mutation. Keep the camera fixed through depth play.

Initial timing hypotheses, to test rather than treat as truths: 180–300 ms for lexical shortening; 500–800 ms for repetition contracting into a loop; about 0.8–1.2 s for the first complete triangle phrase. The visitor may hold every stable state indefinitely. Reduced motion uses immediate state changes and static comparisons.

## 4. Storyboard: the nine forms

### 1 — A gesture

```text
forward 500
left 120
```

**Picture:** a point sets off, leaving one edge. At its end, the small heading turns through an exterior angle of 120°. There is no completed triangle yet.
**Small sentence:** “Go a little way. Turn.”
**Visual responsibility:** make forward and left visible in the picture, not through a glossary. The turning arc distinguishes 120° of turning from the triangle's 60° interior angle.
**Do not:** quietly draw the next side before the source contains it.

### 2 — A return

```text
forward 500
left 120
forward 500
left 120
forward 500
left 120
```

**Picture:** the second and third pairs complete the path. The last turn restores the heading; a small origin witness briefly confirms the return.
**Small sentence:** “Again. And once more.”
**Transition:** the original pair remains. The other pairs arrive as recognisable copies, in the same order as the edges.
**Feeling:** closure, not a fireworks cue.

### 3 — The rule hiding in repetition

```text
loop 3 do
  forward 500
  left 120
end
```

**Picture:** exactly the same triangle, in exactly the same place.
**Small sentence:** “Three times. One little rule.”
**Transition:** lightly group the three pairs; retain one pair at its established baseline; the other two copies withdraw into the count 3. `loop`, `do`, and `end` make a visible enclosure, not a new terminal panel.
**Crucial contrast:** fewer words, unchanged world.
**Optional action:** unfold the loop back into its three explicit copies. This is a presentation of equivalence, not an edit to the running source.

### 4 — A shorter way to say it

```text
loop 3 do
  fw 500
  lt 120
end
```

**Picture:** unchanged.
**Small sentence:** “Same movement. Shorter words.”
**Transition:** `forward` contracts to `fw`; `left` contracts to `lt`. Preserve the remaining letters where possible. No new camera move or triangle animation.
**Hierarchy:** this is a micro-beat, not a grand conceptual climax. Long/short forms require equivalence tests in the actual deployed language.

### 5 — A name you can call

```text
def triangle do
  loop 3 do
    fw 500
    lt 120
  end
end

triangle
```

**Picture:** the already-existing triangle remains as a result/comparison. Defining a body must not be shown as executing it.
**Small sentence:** “Give the movement a name.”
**Transition:** the loop body remains intact while its enclosing definition appears. The call is separated by a small breath of space.
**Optional replay:** the call lights, then its body executes. The definition line does not continuously light merely because the triangle is drawing.

### 6 — A name with room to vary

```text
def triangle length do
  loop 3 do
    fw length
    lt 120
  end
end

triangle 500
```

**Picture:** initially unchanged. After deliberate input, changing the call-site length changes the triangle's size.
**Small sentence:** “How large?”
**Transition:** the 500 in the body is replaced by `length`; 500 appears as the call's argument. Briefly show the relation between those two locations.
**Play:** a visible, labelled length affordance supports one-dimensional direct manipulation. Its exact Paperlang implementation is a compatibility task; do not use an unrelated JavaScript triangle as a stand-in.
**Comparison:** a temporary parent outline makes 500 versus 250 legible; dismiss it after comparison. Do not persist trails of every drag.
**Source correction:** use `length` throughout, not a parameter called `length` and a body referring to `len`.

### 7 — What if the rule calls itself?

```text
def triangle length do
  loop 3 do
    fw length
    lt 120
    triangle length/2
  end
end

triangle 500
```

**Small sentence:** “What if it tries again, half as large?”
**Important truth:** this has no stopping condition. With ordinary depth-first execution, the first recursive call keeps invoking another. It never returns to finish the remaining sides of the enclosing calls. Halving the length does not by itself stop execution.
**Picture:** an honestly labelled finite trace of the first descending branch, with 500 → 250 → 125 → … visible. Do not show a completed, endlessly flowering Sierpiński pattern as this program's output.
**Safety:** never run this unbounded source on the main thread. Use a runtime-supported inspection/stepping boundary; if the host cannot safely yield and stop, show a static, labelled conceptual trace instead. A hidden recursion cap must not masquerade as the source's termination rule.
**Dramatic opening:** this state needs something. The visitor should feel the need to return, not receive an abstract warning about recursion.

### 8 — A stopping rule lets the world unfold

```text
def triangle length step do
  when step>0 do
    loop 3 do
      fw length
      lt 120
      triangle length/2 step-1
    end
  end
end

triangle 500 3
```

**Small sentence:** “Give it a way back.”
**Transition:** introduce `step`, `step-1`, and the guard as one tightly related change. A branch shows 3 → 2 → 1 → 0; at 0 it returns without drawing. The returning cursor can resume its enclosing side sequence.
**Payoff:** the completed recursive figure appears because the branches can now finish. Limitation makes the complexity possible.
**Comparison:** levels 1, 2, 3 are inspectable. Avoid a generational bloom falsely presented as the program's literal depth-first execution.
**Truth of the result:** this draws recursive outlines with retained and sometimes retraced edges, not filled black subtriangles or a stochastic chaos-game construction. Label it a Sierpiński-style recursive triangle outline / finite Sierpiński boundary construction. Do not introduce a fill/removal algorithm without saying the program changed.

### 9 — Put the rule in your hand

Retain the bounded definition. Reveal the controlling relation in a separate small source region:

```text
let X
let X.y=0
fn snap(no, unit) (no - (no // unit))
let depth = snap(X.x, 50)/50
let sierpinski = triangle 500 depth
```

**Small sentence:** “Now it follows your hand.”
**Picture:** X is a visible point constrained to a horizontal rail. No dragging invitation refers to an invisible variable.
**Core gesture:** move the point right; new detail appears inside the fixed outer triangle. Move it left; detail withdraws. Do not rescale the outer triangle to make every depth look equally dense.
**Witness:** a small secondary marker sits at the snapped coordinate. The actual point moves continuously. The depth value changes only when a threshold is crossed.
**Source qualification:** the `//` operator in the currently vendored evaluator is remainder. For nonnegative x, this function snaps down to the lower multiple; it is not ordinary nearest rounding. The original helper's name `round` was misleading. Verify `let`, `fn`, property binding, figure identity, and the malleable figure's reevaluation semantics against the new release before calling this executable on the website.
**Initial position:** request x = 150, depth = 3 through the language-supported initialisation mechanism; its precise source syntax is to be validated, not invented here.
**Bounds:** x ∈ [0, 300], depth ∈ {0, …, 6}. Bounds must be real in the controlling figure/input contract, not just a painted rail. A source-visible or inspectable limit is preferable to a secret saturation in JavaScript.
**At zero:** say “No steps yet” and leave X, the rail, and the controls visible. Blank output is valid, not a failed canvas.
**Last invitation:** “What would you change?” Let the open-in-dojo action carry the actual source and chosen state if the dojo supports that handoff; verify the route/protocol rather than assuming a query-string API.

## 5. The hierarchy of verbs

Keep the site's phrase: **see, play with, breathe to life.** Do not cycle unrelated inspirational words over the code.

- **SEE:** line, turn, repetition, loop, shortening. The visitor can recognise correspondences.
- **PLAY WITH:** naming, length variation, self-reference, stopping. The visitor can compare consequences and ask what if.
- **BREATHE TO LIFE:** the final source is coupled to a visible object they can move.

These are increasing kinds of agency, not ranks of mathematical worth. Earlier stages may allow interaction, and someone may skip directly to play. They are not achievement badges.

Use a quiet persistent phrase. Emphasise the active clause once with brightness and a short underline/positional cue. Do not flash it repeatedly. At the final handoff, “breathe to life” can receive an additional acknowledgement on the first actual adjustment, not claim the visitor acted merely because they scrolled there.

The decisive difference between an animation and a living figure is **a response that belongs to this hand**. No idle breathing animation is needed to manufacture that distinction.

## Three viable visual worlds

### A — The luminous palimpsest — recommended

The existing warm night, one exact luminous stroke, and code that keeps the memory of its earlier arrangement. Escher supplies correspondence; McLaren supplies temporary echoes; Len Lye supplies the stroke's material dignity. The code contracts while its drawing remains; complexity appears inside the same outer boundary.

**Strength:** belongs to Helios without becoming more cinema footage. The live figure becomes the main event.
**Risk:** too much bloom turns the result into decorative generative art. Keep the geometry sharp and the atmosphere quiet.

### B — The paper mechanism

An ivory field, black geometric ink, small coral annotations, and a visible folded-strip logic for grouping/repetition. Klee and Kelly are the closer ancestors. Commands behave like deliberately placed pieces; the loop is a fold, and the controlling point is a movable pin.

**Strength:** very direct, tactile, diagrammatically readable.
**Risk:** an abrupt new daylight theme in the present site; fake paper shadows can become craft cosplay. Choose this only if the interval is deliberately allowed a distinct material surface. Do not combine it indiscriminately with the luminous night.

### C — The metamorphic ribbon

An Escher-like continuous strip: commands become repeated cells, cells contract into a rule, the rule becomes a family of forms. Navigation moves a reading window through a long composition rather than swapping cards.

**Strength:** makes the history of the transformation spatially inspectable.
**Risk:** horizontal reading, mobile layout, and the split between navigation and figure dragging become harder. Better as an expanded editorial companion than the first landing implementation.

The recommended proposal below develops A. B and C remain real alternatives, not extra layers to add to A.

## 6. Art direction: a mathematician's small optical theatre

### Keep the site's atmosphere; change its behaviour

The local Helios prior (`design/prior/helios.jpg`) is an amber shore, atmospheric depth, quiet serif words, and light lingering on water. Keep that warmth and spaciousness. The current code-as-verse idea already supplies a useful alternative to an IDE panel.

**Proposal:** twilight plum/charcoal ground, warm gold established geometry, restrained ember for the active gesture, the existing cool name-ink where appropriate. Use published semantic colour/font roles; any new role belongs in `assets/css/core/tokens.css`, not scattered raw values.

The figure is the brightest, most precise object. The background is still and low contrast. Small diagram annotations disappear after doing their explanatory work.

### Material character

- Stable geometry, clean joins, mathematically exact scale.
- A slight fixed edge character or optical softness on the stroke, never random vertex wobble.
- At most one quiet grain layer; no lens flare, procedural fog, floating equations, paper confetti, or theatrical film scratches.
- Code remains real selectable text. Its warmth comes from type, spacing, grouping, and motion, not from making it illegibly handwritten.
- Use the locally available paperLang face for a very small authored note or wordmark only if it remains legible. Do not require a new handwriting font.
- A recognisable hand-made control may be a small open circle/cross with a short annotation. The interactive hit area is much larger than its drawn footprint.

**Handcrafted means purposeful decisions about every joint, not adding imperfection everywhere.**

### Composition

A single shared field: code on one side, figure on the other, with enough negative space that relations can cross the gap without arrow spaghetti. No editor chrome, segmented card carousel, progress percentage, dashboard counters, giant recursion tree, or persistent coordinate grid.

Code must not shrink as nesting grows. Reserve height for the bounded definition. The final binding may be an explicitly expandable second region, not silently omitted while pretending the visible fragment is the complete program.

On narrow screens: drawing above code, controls below; the active explanation is close to its referent. An initially plausible 300-ish-pixel figure area should be tested on short mobile viewports rather than enforced as a fixed layout truth.

## 7. Progression and page scope

**Prototype recommendation:** a one-stage, nine-state experience with persistent Previous/Next controls and immediate access to the final play state. The page itself scrolls normally. No scroll lock, compulsory timed film, or autoplay on every re-entry.

This preserves the existing interval's one-room character while allowing a leisurely reading. Transitions begin on deliberate navigation; execution demos have a distinct Replay/Stop affordance. A visitor can stop at the tiny triangle, reverse an abstraction, or skip to the malleable figure.

An optional “Let it unfold” presentation can later perform the authored sequence, with pause and cancellation, but it is not the first implementation. The hard problem is meaningful correspondence, not autoplay timing.

If a future version becomes a longer native-scroll essay, ratify that as a changed page-length contract. Do not introduce a many-viewport pinned scroll theatre by accident. Do not ship all the research text into the front-door experience.

Prototype on a dedicated probe surface before substituting for `_includes/home/see.html`. The homepage currently uses films. Do not turn this proposal into an unreviewed replacement of the whole landing page or `/turtle`.

## 8. Three different clocks

1. **Version progression:** which canonical source form the visitor is inspecting. Owned by the page's navigation state.
2. **Rewrite presentation:** token/group motion bridging source versions. Owned by the page, cancellable, not execution evidence.
3. **Program execution / figure response:** what the runtime actually computes and displays. Owned by Paperlang.

`onLine` in the inspected site boundary supplies source lines and logical time, not a complete call stack, live argument ledger, seek control, or figure interaction channel. The spec must not conjure those features out of callbacks that do not contain them.

Represent rewrite bridges as presentation-only; compile/run only canonical source. Keep the last committed source/result pair coherent while a new state is pending. Never label a calculated story timer as the executing source line.

Do not rebuild a miniature evaluator in page JavaScript to simulate Paperlang. A separate small mathematical model may generate test expectations, but not the production drawing or fabricated runtime events.

## 9. Input contract and performance

The malleable figure needs a verified runtime-owned path for input, reevaluation, and cancellation. This section specifies behaviours, not invented API method names.

- One active figure, one controlling identity X, one source of truth for X.x and depth.
- Pointer, keyboard, and labelled native range/number control reach the same underlying input state.
- Pointer capture retains the gesture; release/cancel releases it. Scope gesture handling to the control, not the entire canvas or page.
- Use at least a 44 × 44 CSS-pixel target for the small drawn handle; no hover-only discovery.
- Show a visible label such as “Drag X for more detail” and a keyboard hint where appropriate.
- The rail can map a short screen distance to [0, 300] world units; screen pixels are not the mathematical units. Resize does not alter the selected depth.
- Request reevaluation only when snapped depth changes. Coalesce pending requests; newest input wins. An older completion cannot repaint over a newer state.
- Rapid input must not enqueue one long performance per crossed depth. The immediate response is the selected geometry; slow explanatory replay is a separate mode.
- During a direct drag, no mandatory rewind and redrawing from the first edge on every update.
- No permanently running render loop after settlement merely for decoration. Dispose resources and subscriptions when abandoned; retain or explicitly reset state on return rather than doing so accidentally.
- If the host cannot meet the interactive contract, keep an honest static/film fallback and the readable source. Do not ship a fake JavaScript toy under the same code.

For the guarded outline at depth d ≥ 0, the count of triangle calls that draw is:

```text
T(d) = (3^d - 1) / 2
forward operations = 3 T(d)
```

Depth 3 draws 13 triangle outlines and executes 39 forwards; depth 6 draws 364 outlines and executes 1,092 forwards. Counts exclude turns, guards, loop overhead, zero-step calls, and runtime rendering work. Profile total cost on real devices before increasing the cap.

These results and restoration of the turtle's position/heading were checked with an independent scalar 2D turtle calculation for depths 0–6. That is mathematical evidence, not a Paperlang/browser conformance test. The actual deployed runtime still needs end-to-end checks.

## 10. Accessibility and honest fallbacks

- All nine forms remain inspectable as real text without animation. Provide a static readable sequence/fallback; do not hide the whole explanation behind a canvas.
- Reduced motion disables token travel, drawing sweeps, afterimage choreography, and camera animation. It does not automatically mean “no interaction”: permit discrete chosen depth states where the page/runtime loading policy allows an explicit opt-in. The current hatch policy skips its automatic import under reduced motion and Save-Data; changing that policy requires an explicit decision.
- No flashing. Colour is reinforced by text, position, outline, or selection markers. Check actual contrast, including dim source states.
- Preserve focus across source rewrites. Do not animate/recreate the focused control out from under the visitor.
- Give depth a meaningful accessible label and min/max/current value. Announce discrete chosen states, not every raw pointer sample or every recursive instruction.
- Provide keyboard operation and a non-drag control. The drawn X should not be the only way to change detail.
- A depth-zero result and an unavailable runtime must have different explanations.
- The fallback must describe what is not available. A screenshot is not a playable figure; a film is not current input.

## 11. Compatibility questions to settle, not hide

1. Do current `forward`/`left` genuinely alias `fw`/`lt`? The inspected site spike's command table has the short forms. Do not add a page-side translator and call it the same language.
2. Does the new host support the reported `let X`, properties, function definition, figure identity, and reactive relation exactly as written? Validate each canonical snippet.
3. What initialises X to the desired x without breaking its malleability?
4. What enforces the horizontal constraint and x bounds? Painted geometry alone is not a constraint.
5. Can the runtime yield/stop a trace of unguarded recursion safely? If not, use a clearly identified static conceptual illustration for state 7.
6. What observation protocol, if any, exposes call depth, arguments, return, and geometry identity? Without it, source-line light alone is insufficient for detailed dynamic annotations.
7. Does the runtime provide stable geometry identities across depth reevaluations? If not, begin with immediate correct redraws; do not invent a misleading branch correspondence to obtain a cinematic morph.
8. What is the supported handoff to the dojo? The known homepage destination is a welcome page, not evidence of a source-import protocol.

## 12. Prototype order and acceptance

### First: the two decisive moments

A. **Six lines → one loop:** code contracts; triangle does not change; reversing reveals the correspondence.
B. **X → detail:** the control follows the hand, the outside stays fixed, and more/less detail is immediate and reversible.

If those feel sterile or confusing, more stages and richer texture will not rescue the piece.

### Next: the dramatic hinge

C. Compare an honestly bounded trace of unguarded descent with the guarded program returning and completing. This requires either runtime trace support or clearly labelled static comparison.

### Then: complete the nine forms

Add extension, alias shortening, encapsulation, and parameterisation. Use actual canonical source fixtures. Add the quiet verb hierarchy and material treatment only after correspondence and interaction work.

### Gates

- Each canonical supported snippet compiles/runs on the selected release, without a site dialect.
- The first state draws one edge and rotates 120°, not a secretly completed triangle.
- States 2–6, at length 500, agree geometrically and restore the expected pose for closed triangles; alias equivalence is tested.
- State 7 cannot lock up or misrepresent a cap as natural termination.
- Guarded depth 0 does nothing; depth 1 is a triangle; depth 3 matches the expected 13 outline calls; all completed calls restore their entry pose within tolerance.
- Loop contraction/aliasing/naming do not rescale or alter the drawing.
- X.x = 0, 49, 50, 149, 150, 300 produce depths 0, 0, 1, 2, 3, 6 under the nonnegative remainder rule.
- Keyboard and pointer produce the same selected depth; snapping is visible; bounds are real; cancel/resize/rapid reversal do not leave stale drawings or captured gestures.
- No automatic advancement occurs while someone is manipulating or reading a held state.
- Reduced-motion, Save-Data, no-JS, loading failure, depth-zero, and offscreen/disposal states are deliberate and distinguishable.
- Probe the page for source/result coherence, mobile layout, actual contrast, and leaks before moving the homepage. Existing surface/style/media rules still apply.

### Test with people, not only timings

Hypotheses to test with a small initial group, including a non-programmer and a child with appropriate consent/support:

- After the first turn, can they indicate the next heading?
- After contraction, can they explain why the picture did not change?
- Can they tell a definition from the call that makes it execute?
- At the recursion hinge, can they explain why halving alone was insufficient?
- Do they discover X without a spoken prompt, and can they predict what moving it left will do?
- Do they reverse, compare, or formulate a new question rather than only admire the final result?

Do not treat small-group observations as statistical evidence. Use them to locate confusion and revise the correspondence.

## 13. Secondary explanatory priors

The visual lineage above leads. These references keep its mechanics humane.

- [Bret Victor, Learnable Programming](https://worrydream.com/LearnableProgramming/): make vocabulary, flow, and state visible; abstract from concrete examples. Particularly important here: a finished picture beside source is not itself an explanation of execution.
- [Nicky Case, How I Make Explorable Explanations](https://blog.ncase.me/how-i-make-an-explorable-explanation/): begin with a question and a concrete experience, ascend through connected consequences, finish with questions of the visitor's own. This supports the turn from halving to a stopping rule, not a list of unrelated language features.
- [Amit Patel, Little Design Things](https://www.redblobgames.com/making-of/little-things/): semantic correspondence, limited emphasis, readable controls, expanded hit targets, touch and platform conventions. Do not spend all the colours at once.
- [Mimsy Were the Borogoves, the supplied transcription](https://jabberwockland.blogspot.com/2007/03/mimsy-were-borogoves-by-lewis-padgett.html): a strange toy becomes interesting through use before its larger possibilities are explained. Borrow that invitation and progression, not the story's coercion, punishments, or fictional claims about infant psychology. Paperlang's causality should become clearer, not remain a mystical black box.

## The final design test

> Can someone recognise the original gesture inside the complexity—and feel that the complexity now answers to them?

If yes, the mathematics has not merely been pictured. A relation to it has been made.
