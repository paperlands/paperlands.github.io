The last viewport is the landing's dawn, drawn — not photographed, not filtered, no new asset: two silhouettes of land, a sun going down behind them, the air it lights, and the wet floor that throws it back at the reader. The reference is design/prior/helios.jpg, and the three things that make that frame a dawn are structural, not decorative: 1. THE HORIZON IS NOT A LINE. It is the upper contour of land that is darker than the sky behind it. Nothing is stroked along it: the sun's disc is cut by the near ridge, and that cut IS the horizon. An earlier version of this close drew a hairline arc with a glow around it, which is the same picture inverted — a bright line on a dark ground reads as a rule with light, never as weather. 2. THE LIGHT COMES TOWARD THE READER. The sun's path lies on the floor, not in the sky: a 24-unit opening under the ridge that widens to 414 units at the bottom edge, tapering on Bézier edges, broken into a few separate lips so it reads as light on water rather than as a road. The gaps are the point, and there are deliberately few of them. 3. NOTHING MOVES SIDEWAYS. A dawn is a sustained illumination, not a light travelling across a line, so the crossing glint and its `stroke-dashoffset` are gone — that animation also repainted the whole band every frame for one thin dash. What is left to move is the grain the surface already carries and the doors' own breath below. The SVG covers the section (viewBox 1200x720, `slice`, anchored to the bottom) so the floor always meets the page's last pixel, whatever the viewport's aspect: the sun keeps its 40% of the height, the ridge its 46%, the water its rest. Nothing is clipped into view by a hidden overflow. Colours are read from the site's light roles, never stated here: each `` names a role with `data-ink` and components/helios.css binds it.
