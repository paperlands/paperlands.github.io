#+title: Video as a first-class page
#+subtitle: A small language so Helios, and every later page, compose performantly with first class web experience

* Aim

Video is a page material, not a special case.

A page names clips. Surfaces place them. One player attaches and
releases decoders. Helios is the first full expression of that
language, not a second runtime.

The page must stay seamless when many clips exist, and cheap when
they do not all need to run. Poster paints immediately. Motion is
a privilege the player grants.

If an author must mention =src=, =preload=, or =play()=, the language
has failed for *flow*. Carousels may use one shared writer. Nothing
else.

* Vocabulary

Three nouns. Two marks. One carousel. A global cap.

** Clip

A named asset in =_data/media.yml=.

Required fields:

- =src=     — H.264 MP4, faststart, silent, box-sized. This is coverage.
- =poster=  — the still that *is* the page (own layer, not only the
              video =poster= attribute)
- =width=, =height= — of =src=, so the box cannot collapse
- =alt=

Optional: =av1= — same pixels as =src=, fewer bytes. Not a second size.
Not required for any browser. Explicit include params override the
catalog.

Optional: =compact_src=, =compact_av1=, =compact_width=, =compact_height= —
the phone portrait of the same clip. One extra rung for a full-bleed portrait
surface, not a device ladder. Minted by =design/mint-media.mjs=; selected
below 640px. A clip without a compact pair uses its desktop pair everywhere.

A clip without a poster is not a clip.

** Surface

How a clip sits. Surfaces do not play.

- *box*   — inline, aspect locked, still behind the video
- *fill*  — full-bleed behind copy (masthead)
- *plate* — stacked carousel cell (Helios "how we")
- *pane*  — strip carousel cell (Helios "who we become")

New looks are new surfaces over the same clip. They do not grow
new players.

** Carousel

Stage and reel are not two runtimes. They are one behaviour in two
surfaces.

A carousel is a *group* of clips with one active member and decoded
neighbours. It advances on =ended= (clips are not =loop=) or on
control (rail, swipe). When the carousel's root leaves the viewport,
it clears its marks so the cap can serve something else.

| kind  | surface | layout                         | decode set            |
|-------+---------+--------------------------------+-----------------------|
| stack | plate   | overlapping, same viewport     | active + next         |
| strip | pane    | horizontal sequence            | previous + active + next |

Flow (grids, masthead) is not a carousel. No writer. Near attaches,
far releases, under the same cap.

The writer is the only extra JavaScript. It sets marks and calls
=sync()=. It never opens a file. Wrap clones (infinite strip) mark
*one* live node, not the clone.

** Marks

The scarce thing is a decoder. Speak that.

| mark     | meaning                                       |
|----------+-----------------------------------------------|
| =decode= | this clip may hold a decoder (=src= attached) |
| =active= | this clip is the one playing                  |

=active= implies =decode=. Decode without active is the neighbour.
Neither is poster only.

Marks live on a *group*. After they change, =paperlandMedia.sync()=.

Flow writes no marks.

Do not add =keep=. Do not add =warm=. Do not add a mode per look.

* Invariants

1. *Poster first, as a layer.* A still sits *behind* the video
   (image layer). The video is opacity 0 until it is actually playing.
   Unbind must not reveal a black frame. Reduced motion, save-data, and
   failed autoplay stay on the still.

   After a clip has played, the still *is* its last decoded frame: the
   player paints that frame onto the poster layer on =ended= and before
   unbind, then drops ready. Next may start; this one keeps its picture.
   The catalog poster is the still until a frame has been seen. A clip
   without an opening poster is not a clip.

2. *Few decoders, globally.* At most *three* attached videos on the
   page. Carousel writers *and* flow share this cap. Visible + active
   win; then decode neighbours; then flow that is near. When a
   strip is in view it will usually hold the cap; the stack must
   have cleared =active=. Writers still clear on leave — the cap is
   the safety net, not the choreography.

3. *Unbind is the release.* Pause is not enough. Capture the still,
   remove sources, =load()=, drop ready. The still remains.
4. *One player.* =assets/js/media-video.js= via =media/boot.html=.
   One carousel writer later. Gallery, raw =<video autoplay>=, and
   page-local players are dialects to retire.

5. *Markup does not play.* Includes emit tags and data. Playback
   lives in the player. Carousels only toggle marks.

6. *The catalog is the type.* Pages pass =id=. Paths in pages are
   overrides, not the norm.

7. *Encode to the surface.* One H.264 file per clip, sized for how it
   sits, not a device ladder. fps ≤ 30, silent, faststart. No file ≥
   100 MB. No LFS. No HLS. One id, one box: do not reuse a hero file
   as a pane.

* Look

A page declares a *look*. A look binds *slots*. Neither knows the other's
classes.

Four tiers, one direction of dependency:

| tier    | artefact                      | owns                                 |
|---------+-------------------------------+--------------------------------------|
| tokens  | =core/tokens.css= (@theme)    | values, declared once                |
| surface | =core/media.css= + =media/*=  | placement, behaviour, the slots      |
| look    | =components/<look>.css=       | ink, type, air — bound to slots      |
| act     | =_includes/home/*= + the page | copy and order                       |

** Slots

A surface publishes attributes. A look binds those, and nothing else. The
carousel publishes:

| attribute                                     | meaning                       |
|-----------------------------------------------+-------------------------------|
| =data-media-carousel="stack|strip"=            | the behaviour                 |
| =data-media-surface="fill|plate|pane|box"=     | the placement                 |
| =data-media-slot=                             | =frame copy kicker title slate rail= |
| =data-media-to= =data-media-cell= =data-media-track= | the parts              |
| =data-media-active= =data-media-decode=       | the marks the player reads    |

=_includes/nav.html= publishes =data-nav-mark= the same way, so a look can draw its
own identity over the nav's brand link and put the focus ring on something
visible.

A class name is *not* a slot. It is private to the file that emits it and may
be renamed at any time; the slot is the promise.

** =data-media-copy= is not a slot

It is the writer's channel, and it appears only where the writer may write
text — once, in the stack. A strip must never carry it, or the writer would
set every pane's title to the active cell's. =design/probe/looks.py= refuses a
look that binds it.

** Laws

1. A look binds published attributes, never another component's classes.
2. A page declares a look; it never carries one. =_pages/helios.md= has no look
   in it — =_layouts/folio.html= writes =data-look= from front matter.
3. Look-owned runtime state is namespaced: =data-helios-copy=, not =.is-on=,
   which the writer already uses for the rail.
4. One axis, one writer. Nested axes opt in. JS never writes an axis the user
   is driving.

** Page motion

Who may move the page is the same kind of contract as who may bind a slot.

| axis | owner | may write | may only read |
|------+-------+-----------+---------------|
| document y | the page | the user | look clocks (the mark) |
| document y interpolation | document frame (=core/document.css=) | opt-in on =body:not([data-theme]):not([data-shell])= | hash, footnotes |
| nested x | surface (=core/media.css=) | the user; the writer, and only on rail | writer paints from geometry |
| play() jump | player | synchronous undo of /its own/ jump | — |

A look does not snap y. Height is not a scroll job: viewport-tall acts keep
the ticker a seam without becoming snap targets. Native y is the default.
A surface does not write =html=. Fill is 100% of its box; only inside
=.wrapper= does it break out, and the document clips x. =overscroll-behavior=
is the longhand on x only.

DX: to change how the page scrolls, you are on the page (the look) or the
document frame. To change how the reel rests, you are in =media.css=. To
change what plays, you are in the player. If you are in the writer and about
to =scrollTo= on y, stop.

** Layering

A look does not get its own =@layer= sub-layer. A nested layer is subordinate
to its parent, so =@layer components.helios= loses to =@layer components=
whatever the specificity (architecture.org T9). A look lives in =components=
and carries its precedence in specificity. Motion overrides go in
=utilities=, which is a sibling layer *after* =components=.

* Coverage

H.264 MP4 (yuv420p, =+faststart=) plays in every browser we care
about: Safari iOS and macOS, Chrome, Firefox, Edge, Android.

AV1 is an optional smaller sibling at the *same* width and height.
Chrome, Firefox, Edge, and many Androids will take it. Safari
without AV1 uses =src=. That pair is coverage + a gift, not a matrix.

The compact pair is a *rendition*, not a codec: same clip, the portrait box
the phone actually shows, selected by a media query below 640px. It is the
one place where a second size is allowed, because the hero's 1920×1080 frame
is mostly cropped away in a 412×777 box. Tablet and desktop keep the desktop
pair.

VP9 is not required. Do not mint a third codec.

Source order in the player: AV1 (if present), then =src=.

* Files

The player can only be cheap if the files match the surface. A clip has one
desktop pair and, only where the reader holds a portrait box, one phone
rendition. That is not a device matrix: these are the boxes the site actually
has.

| key                          | box                                  | encode                 |
|------------------------------+--------------------------------------+------------------------|
| =src=                        | fill / stack / strip, desktop        | 1920×1080 or 1280×720  |
| =av1=                        | same box, same pixels                | same width/height       |
| =compact_src= / =compact_av1= | full-bleed phone portrait, cover     | 572×1080 from 1920×1080 |
| =poster=                     | the still behind the clip            | long edge 720–1280      |

Pick the larger desktop rung when the master is sharp and the surface is
full-bleed; the smaller when the master is already soft or the pane is the
only home. A strip pane is minted at its own portrait box and does not need a
compact pair. The compact crop is the one =object-fit: cover= already shows,
so mobile keeps the composition it had — it stops paying for the pixels the
crop throws away.

** Mint

=design/mint-media.mjs= is the pipeline. It probes the master, encodes H.264
and AV1, extracts the still, hashes the bytes into the filename, and writes
the catalog fields. One command, no remembered ffmpeg incantations. The
minting tools are local, not CI: ffmpeg with libx264 and libsvtav1, and
ImageMagick for the poster still. CI does not encode clips. Responsive stills
are not minted; the build emits them.

#+begin_src sh
node design/mint-media.mjs video \
  --id tree_fractal \
  --source design/assets/HOWSTAR.mp4 \
  --compact 572x1080 \
  --catalog _data/media.yml
#+end_src

=--compact-only= adds the phone pair to an existing desktop pair without
re-encoding it. =--dry-run= prints the ffmpeg work. Desktop: H.264 / CRF 28 /
slow / =+faststart= and AV1 / CRF 38 / preset 8. Compact: H.264 / CRF 28 and
AV1 / CRF 46 — the smaller surface hides the difference; SSIM against the
reference crop stayed above .984 on the tested clip. Audio is removed: the
site's clips are silent. Names carry the first eight hex of the file hash, so
a re-mint is idempotent and a catalog entry always names the bytes it ships.

Images are a different contract. A still is one committed source plus the
widths the layout needs, in =_data/image_sources.yml=. =_plugins/image_variants.rb=
runs that recipe during =jekyll build= and =jekyll serve=: it encodes the rungs
into =.jekyll-cache/image-variants/=, fills =site.data.image_variants= before
render, and copies the files into the destination after Jekyll's cleaner.
=_includes/media/img.html= reads that manifest, so a page asks for an image
by the path it already names and gets =srcset= when the path is in the recipe.
The source's own width is always the largest candidate, so a 2x display never
upscales a small rung. A WebP source is that candidate itself; a PNG is emitted
as a WebP of the same pixels. The helper defaults to =loading="lazy"=; callers
mark the hero still =eager=/=high= and below-fold art =low=.

The rung's name is the first eight hex of a hash of the source bytes, the
width, and the encoder version. It is not a hash of the output. Output bytes
are not stable across ImageMagick builds, which is why the build does not
shell out to whatever =magick= the runner has. The encoder is =sharp=, pinned
in =package-lock.json= (Node 20). A lockfile bump renames the rungs. A repeat
build encodes nothing. =npm ci= once, then Jekyll. A missing =sharp=, a missing
source, or an unsafe path fails the build. There is no fallback that ships
the full file for a recipe entry. The resized files are not committed.

Verify before push:

#+begin_src sh
node design/mint-media.mjs verify
#+end_src

It fails on a missing catalog path, a missing image source, a compact clip
without its box, or two byte-identical =.woff2= files. The font generator
(=design/probe/webfonts.py=) collapses identical weights onto one URL; verify
is the tripwire if someone adds a duplicate by hand.
* Host

This site is Jekyll 4 on Actions (=deploy-pages=). =_plugins/= run.
That does not mean we encode in Ruby.

** How we ship a clip

1. *Mint before push.* The commit contains the poster, the H.264, the
   optional AV1 sibling and, when the surface is a phone portrait, the
   compact pair. CI does not encode; it runs =node design/mint-media.mjs
   verify=.

2. *No LFS. No file at 100 MB.* Git's ceiling is a wall, not a budget.

3. *Content-address the served file.* =waves-7f3a2c9d.mp4=. Re-encode
   → new name → catalog id points at it. The name is the cache key.
   =mint-media.mjs= writes eight hex characters from the file's SHA-256.

4. *Cache what this visit fetched.* Workbox must not precache
   =mp4=/=webm=. Precache shell + posters. Runtime may CacheFirst a
   handful of hashed loops the player actually attached
   (=maxEntries= at the cap).

** Use from Jekyll / Tailwind

- =_data/media.yml= is the catalog.
- =_data/image_sources.yml= is the still recipe. The build fills
  =site.data.image_variants=; pages use =media/img.html=, not a hand-written
  srcset. The resized files are not committed.
- =mint-media.mjs verify= runs in CI: a missing clip, a missing still source,
  or a duplicate font byte fails the build. The image encoder failing closed
  is the build itself.
- Includes compose surfaces. A generator is for notes, not clips.
- =relative_url= on every local src.
- Include locals leak: =media_*= names only.
- Fill height is a CSS slot (=--media-fill-h= / =--media-fill-h-md=),
  written by Liquid as data, not composed as =h-[…]= classes.
** Do not use

- ffmpeg in the Jekyll build (clips stay minted)
- ImageMagick, or any unpinned encoder, for stills
- committing the resized WebP rungs
- Git LFS
- jekyll-youtube / embed plugins
- jekyll-picture-tag as a video model
- Workbox glob on video
- VP9 as the spine
- A second decoder script for "the carousel"

** Limits

Published site ≤ 1 GB; ~100 GB bandwidth / month (soft). =design/=
must be =exclude=d or Helios masters ship. Byte-range works on
Pages; faststart matters. =play()= on iOS can jump scroll — the
player restores it.

* What Helios is

Helios is a *page*: verse, wordmark, grain, ticker, plus two
carousels.

- Wordmark, grain, ticker, verse — not this language
- *Stack carousel* — "In how we": five plates, no rail (the ticker names them),
  the action lowercase on the verse's baseline
- *Strip carousel* — "In who we become": six panes

Express it by composing catalog + surfaces + the carousel writer.
Do not port the 1.2k-line file. Look that is Helios lives beside the
includes (folio theme binds; page CSS for verse), not inside =media/=.

That is now realised. =_pages/helios.md= has no =<video>=, no =src=, no
=preload= and no =play()=; it names copy in its front matter and ids from
=_data/media.yml=, and calls one include per act. The look lives in
=assets/css/components/helios.css=, bound by =data-look= on <body>; the
page-local decoration is =assets/js/home/helios.js=, which reads the marks
and the =media:carousel= event the writer already publishes and writes no
mark and opens no file.

The *expression* is the reference prototype's, and it is asserted rather than
described: =design/probe/helios.mjs= checks that the mark builds by scroll
distance, that the reel dissolves at its edges, that the plate is graded, that
the title is masked rather than faded, and that the mark is whole once the act
is past.

| the reference does                                            | where it lives now |
|---------------------------------------------------------------+--------------------|
| grain, silk veil                                              | =home/air.html= + look |
| the mark assembling — columns, roof, sun, rays — on scroll    | =home/wordmark.html= + look + page-local JS |
| hero stage: still under film, cross-fade, scrim               | =media/carousel.html= + look |
| the sun flash — once, when the sun is up and the mark stands  | =home/wordmark.html= + look + page-local JS |
| the thesis, and a card lifted rather than faded               | =home/hero.html= + look |
| the play fill — no rail; the ticker names the clips           | the writer's marks + look + JS |
| the ember ticker                                              | =home/ticker.html= + look |
| the reel: edge dissolve, pane frame and wash, pane meta       | =home/who.html= + look |
| the close                                                     | =home/colophon.html= + look |

One reference move is now realised without the reference's transform loop: the
reel's infinite wrap with clone edge panes. The strip stays a native horizontal
scroller with =scroll-snap-type: x mandatory= — which is what makes it work on a
phone with no JavaScript at all — so the edge panes are still-only clones and
the seam is a scroll reposition: resting on a clone lands on its identical
original. The row opens on the middle identity and wraps in both directions
(=assets/js/home/helios-who.js=). The contract anticipated it: a wrap clone
"marks one live node, not the clone".

The wordmark is the one place Helios ships raster art of its own: the reference's
pieces, 92 kB, in =assets/lib/helios-wordmark/=. They are the brand; redrawing
them as vectors would be inventing brand geometry.

Three ergonomics worth keeping:

- *The copy slot is the card.* =media/carousel.html= emits =data-media-slot=
  ="kicker|title|slate"= and the writer writes through =data-media-copy=. Two
  attributes, two owners: the slot is presentation, the channel is playback.
  Helios binds the slot and never mentions the channel. A page that needs a
  *different* copy shape still has no slot — the sanctioned escape hatch is
  ={% capture %}= into a param, not new markup in =media/=.
- *A page-level scrim cannot sit under the carousel's own copy.* The
  carousel is an isolated stacking context, so an overlay authored in the
  page paints above every slot inside it. The scrim is therefore scoped to
  the look and applied to the fill box as =::after= at =z-index: 1=, below
  =media.css='s =z-index: 2= copy and rail.
- *A horizontal scroller is also a vertical one.* =overflow-x: auto= forces
  =overflow-y= to compute to =auto=, so every strip is a vertical scroll
  container with nothing to scroll — permanently at its boundary. The
  =overscroll-behavior= *shorthand* therefore refuses the vertical axis, and the
  page stops dead under the pointer with content still below. Use
  =overscroll-behavior-x: contain=: it keeps the intent (a horizontal swipe must
  not trigger browser back/forward) and lets the other axis through.
  =design/probe/scroll.mjs= holds it.
- *Library hover/focus ink is scoped to documents without a theme.* Folio does
  not inherit it. The colophon seal does not need a span to escape it.

* Acceptance

1. Helios (preview or homepage) runs on the shared player and writer.
2. A strip pane that has played stays on its last frame when the next
   starts and after unbind. An unplayed pane shows the catalog poster.
3. Off-screen clips unbind; the cap holds on a phone.
4. Save-data and reduced motion show stills only, everywhere.
5. A flow page adds a clip by catalog id and a surface, with no new
   JavaScript. A carousel page uses the writer, not a new player.
6. Safari plays =src= (H.264). Chrome may play AV1. Nobody needs VP9.

Until (1) and (2), we have plumbing. Until Helios is a page of two
carousels without a second decoder, we do not yet have the language
on the homepage.
